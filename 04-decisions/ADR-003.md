# ADR-003

## Title

Derived Artifacts Are Disposable

---

## Status

Accepted

---

## Date

2026-08-06

---

# Context

The platform transforms canonical legal knowledge into multiple derived artifacts to improve retrieval performance.

Examples include:

- retrieval chunks
- embeddings
- keyword indexes
- vector indexes
- search caches
- retrieval metadata

These artifacts accelerate query processing but do not introduce new legal knowledge.

Treating derived artifacts as persistent business data would introduce unnecessary operational complexity:

- migration overhead
- synchronization issues
- backup costs
- version inconsistencies
- difficult parser upgrades

The platform therefore requires a clear distinction between canonical knowledge and generated artifacts.

---

# Decision

Only canonical knowledge shall be considered persistent business data.

All derived artifacts shall be reproducible.

Derived artifacts may be deleted and regenerated at any time without loss of legal information.

The regeneration process shall be deterministic for identical inputs and processing versions.

---

# Canonical Data

The following objects are canonical:

- Original Document
- Document Version
- Knowledge Tree
- Metadata Registry

Canonical objects require:

- persistence
- backup
- versioning
- integrity verification

---

# Derived Artifacts

The following objects are derived:

- Chunks
- Embeddings
- Keyword Indexes
- Vector Indexes
- Retrieval Cache
- Temporary Search Results

Derived artifacts require:

- reproducibility
- version tracking
- regeneration capability

Derived artifacts do not require:

- business versioning
- long-term archival
- independent backup

---

# Consequences

## Positive

Storage architecture becomes simpler.

Parser upgrades become safer.

Embedding models may be replaced freely.

Search engines become replaceable.

Disaster recovery becomes simpler.

Operational costs decrease.

---

## Negative

Rebuilding derived artifacts may require processing time.

Large document collections may require background re-indexing.

Temporary search performance may decrease during rebuild.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Alternative A

Persist every generated artifact.

Rejected.

Reason:

Introduces unnecessary operational complexity.

Generated artifacts can always be recreated.

---

## Alternative B

Persist embeddings only.

Rejected.

Reason:

Embeddings depend on the selected embedding model.

Changing models would invalidate stored vectors.

---

## Alternative C

Treat all retrieval infrastructure as disposable.

Accepted.

Reason:

Canonical knowledge remains the only authoritative source.

Infrastructure becomes replaceable.

---

# Architectural Impact

Knowledge Layer owns canonical data.

Knowledge Processing Pipeline owns derived artifacts.

Replacing:

- embedding model
- chunking strategy
- vector database
- keyword engine

does not require changes to canonical knowledge.

---

# Invariants

Canonical knowledge is never regenerated from derived artifacts.

Derived artifacts are always regenerated from canonical knowledge.

Deleting derived artifacts never causes legal information loss.

Original documents remain immutable.

Knowledge Trees remain immutable within a document version.

---

# Disaster Recovery

System recovery follows the sequence:

Original Documents

↓

Knowledge Trees

↓

Chunk Generation

↓

Embedding Generation

↓

Index Generation

↓

Ready

Canonical knowledge is sufficient to reconstruct the complete retrieval infrastructure.

---

# Future Considerations

Future retrieval technologies may introduce additional derived artifacts.

These artifacts shall follow the same lifecycle.

No derived artifact may become the system of record.

---

# Rationale

Separating canonical knowledge from derived artifacts simplifies architecture, reduces operational complexity and enables continuous evolution of retrieval technologies.

The system preserves only legal knowledge.

Everything else is an optimization.
