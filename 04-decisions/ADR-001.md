# ADR-001

## Title

Canonical Knowledge Representation

---

## Status

Accepted

---

## Date

2026-08-06

---

# Context

The Banking Legal Platform processes legal documents originating from official sources.

A single document passes through multiple stages:

Document

↓

OCR

↓

Parsing

↓

Knowledge Organization

↓

Chunking

↓

Embedding

↓

Retrieval

↓

Generation

Without a canonical representation, every downstream component would be forced to interpret documents independently.

This leads to:

- duplicated parsing logic
- inconsistent hierarchy
- inconsistent citations
- non-deterministic retrieval
- difficult debugging
- poor traceability

The system therefore requires one representation that every downstream component can trust.

---

# Decision

The Knowledge Tree is adopted as the canonical representation of legal knowledge.

Every downstream artifact shall be derived from the Knowledge Tree.

No downstream component shall interpret original documents directly.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

Single source of truth.

Consistent legal hierarchy.

Deterministic parsing.

Consistent citations.

Rebuildable search indexes.

Simplified debugging.

Independent module evolution.

---

## Negative

Parser quality becomes critical.

Parser upgrades require document reprocessing.

Knowledge Tree versioning becomes mandatory.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Alternative A

Retrieve directly from parsed text.

Rejected.

Reason:

Different retrieval components would create different interpretations of document structure.

---

## Alternative B

Retrieve directly from PDF.

Rejected.

Reason:

PDF layout is presentation-oriented rather than semantic.

Scanned documents further increase ambiguity.

---

## Alternative C

Use chunks as the canonical representation.

Rejected.

Reason:

Chunks are retrieval artifacts.

Chunk boundaries may change as retrieval strategies evolve.

Chunks cannot reliably preserve complete legal hierarchy.

---

## Alternative D

Use embeddings as canonical knowledge.

Rejected.

Reason:

Embeddings are model-dependent.

They contain no explicit legal structure.

They cannot reconstruct original documents.

---

# Architectural Impact

The following components consume the Knowledge Tree:

- Chunk Generator
- Embedding Pipeline
- Index Builder
- Retrieval Engine
- Citation Builder

The following components never access original documents directly:

- Retrieval
- Generation

Original documents remain archival sources only.

---

# Invariants

Original documents are immutable.

Knowledge Trees are canonical.

Chunks are derived artifacts.

Embeddings are derived artifacts.

Indexes are derived artifacts.

Every answer must be traceable back to the Knowledge Tree.

---

# Future Considerations

Future parser improvements may produce newer versions of the Knowledge Tree.

Derived artifacts may be regenerated at any time.

Canonical knowledge remains stable throughout the lifetime of a document version.

---

# Rationale

Legal information systems require deterministic behaviour.

A canonical representation minimizes ambiguity, improves traceability and enables independent evolution of downstream components without changing business semantics.

This decision establishes the architectural foundation for every subsequent module in the platform.
